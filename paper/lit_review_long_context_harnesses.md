# Long-context harnesses: a literature review

*Prepared 2026-09-21 for the ICLR 2027 submission. Every work below was checked against its arXiv page, proceedings page, or primary post on the date above; venues are given where a proceedings record exists, otherwise the arXiv date. Cite keys in brackets match `paper/iclr2027/iclr2027_conference.bib`.*

## 0. Scope and terms

A **harness** here means an inference-time procedure that lets a language model with a fixed context window act on an input longer than that window, by controlling what enters the window: retrieving, chunking, summarizing, keeping an external memory, or delegating to further model calls. It is the third of three routes to long documents, next to **native context scaling** (training the model to attend over a longer window) and **architectural memory** (changing the attention mechanism or adding a recurrent state inside the model). The review covers harnesses; the other two appear only as the contrast that motivates them.

Two axes organize the harness literature and matter for our positioning:

1. **Aggregation structure.** How partial results over pieces of the input are combined: retrieve-then-read, parallel map-reduce or tree-reduce, sequential fold over a running memory, interactive navigation, or recursive self-invocation where the model itself decides the structure.
2. **Prompted versus trained.** Whether the harness policy is a hand-written scaffold around a frozen model, or a behavior trained into the model's weights, and if trained, from what supervision: human feedback, trajectories from a stronger model, reinforcement learning on outcomes, or scripted oracles on synthetic tasks.

## 1. Why harnesses: claimed context versus effective context

The case for harnesses rests on a consistent empirical finding that a model's usable context is much shorter than its advertised window. Liu et al. showed that answer position inside a long prompt changes accuracy, with a pronounced dip for information in the middle [liu2024lostmiddle]. RULER built a synthetic suite spanning retrieval, multi-hop tracing, aggregation, and QA, and found that most models claiming 32K or more fall short of that length on anything beyond literal retrieval [hsieh2024ruler]. NoLiMa removed the lexical-overlap shortcut from needle tests and found most 128K-plus models below 50% of their short-context score by 32K tokens [modarressi2025nolima]. BABILong estimated that popular models use 10 to 20 percent of their context on reasoning-in-a-haystack tasks [kuratov2024babilong]; HELMET argued for application-centric evaluation to 128K and found needle tests uninformative about downstream quality [yen2025helmet]; LongBench v2 pushed realistic multiple-choice tasks to 2M words, where the best model at the time barely exceeded a human baseline [bai2024longbenchv2]. Chroma's Context Rot study ran 18 frontier models through controlled experiments and found every one degrades with input length even on copying tasks, well before the window is full [chroma2025contextrot]. OOLONG isolates the aggregation regime specifically: questions that require classifying many entries and then computing a distributional answer, which frontier models fail at modest lengths [bertsch2025oolong]. This is the regime our paper targets.

Xu et al. give the degradation a theory. They decompose a long-context failure into task noise (cross-chunk dependence), model noise (confusion growing with input length), and aggregator noise (imperfect merging of partials), and prove that when model noise grows superlinearly with length, chunk-based processing with a planner, parallel workers, and a manager lets a weaker model beat a stronger single-shot model; experimentally, chunked Llama-3-70B and Qwen-72B match or beat single-shot GPT-4o on long inputs [xu2025divideconquer]. The three noise terms map directly onto the three things a harness must get right: pick pieces that don't cut dependencies, keep each piece short enough that the model stays accurate, and merge without loss.

Native scaling meanwhile has produced ever larger claimed windows: position interpolation and YaRN extended pretrained models by rescaling rotary positions [chen2023pi, peng2024yarn]; LongRoPE reached 2M tokens [ding2024longrope]; Ring Attention made training at such lengths feasible [liu2024ringattention]; recipes for continued pretraining to 128K and 512K followed [xiong2023effective, fu2024dataengineering, gao2025prolong]; Gemini 1.5 shipped 1M to 2M tokens with 10M in its report [geminiteam2024gemini15]; and vendor claims now reach 10M (Llama 4 Scout, Pokee-Isaac 28B), 12M (SubQ), and 100M (Magic's LTM-2-mini) [meta2025llama4, zhu2026pokeeisaac, dangel2026subq, magic2024ltm2mini]. Architectural memory approaches keep a bounded state inside the model, for example attention sinks with a sliding window [xiao2023streamingllm] or compressive memory in each layer [munkhdalai2024infini]. The gap between these claims and the effective-context findings above is the space harnesses occupy: they trade a fixed, small per-call context for more calls, and their accuracy is bounded by the model's short-context accuracy rather than by its long-context degradation curve.

## 2. Retrieval-first harnesses

The oldest harness is retrieve-then-read. RAPTOR made retrieval itself hierarchical, building a tree of recursive summaries so that a query can pull passages at several levels of abstraction [sarthi2024raptor]. Self-Route compared RAG against full-context prompting across datasets and proposed routing each query by model self-reflection, recovering most of the full-context quality at a fraction of the cost [li2024selfroute]. Yue et al. showed that long-context RAG has inference scaling laws of its own when retrieval is combined with in-context demonstrations and iterative decomposition, with near-linear gains under optimal compute allocation [yue2025inferencescaling]. LC-Boost argued that most long-context tasks are "short-context solvable" and had a short-context model decide, per query, how to access and then how to use pieces of the input [qian2024lcboost].

The limitation for our setting is structural: retrieval returns a subset, and aggregation questions need every item. Chain of Agents makes exactly this criticism, that input reduction "has no guarantee of covering the part with needed information" [zhang2024coa]. OOLONG's tasks are the clearest case, since a count or a most-common label over a document has no small witness set to retrieve.

## 3. Chunk and aggregate: map-reduce and tree-reduce

The parallel family splits the input into pieces, processes each independently, and merges. The earliest trained instance is Wu et al.'s recursive book summarization, which fine-tuned GPT-3 with behavioral cloning and a reward model to summarize sections, then summaries of summaries, so that human raters could judge the whole without reading the book [wu2021recursive]. Summ^N generalized coarse-to-fine multi-stage summarization for dialogues and documents [zhang2022summn]. Parallel Context Windows achieved the split at the attention level, restricting attention to within each window while reusing position embeddings, a training-free trick that sits between harness and architecture [ratner2023pcw].

The modern versions concentrate on what passes between map and reduce. LLM×MapReduce defines a structured information protocol (extracted facts, rationale, answer, confidence) and an in-context confidence calibration step, and names the two failure modes it is fighting: inter-chunk dependency and inter-chunk conflict [zhou2024mapreduce]. ToM organizes the reduce along the document's own hierarchy, generating rationales at the leaves and resolving conflicts upward [guo2025tom]. Ou and Lapata show that hierarchical merging hallucinates unless the merge is re-grounded in source context, and add replacement, refinement, and alignment steps to fix it [ou2025hierarchical]. XpandA partitions dynamically according to input length, updates a centralized shared memory through a question-guided protocol, and selectively replays partitions, reporting gains from 1K to 1M tokens over full-context, RAG, and earlier agent methods [xiao2025xpanda]. Xu et al.'s planner-worker-manager framework is the same shape with the theory attached [xu2025divideconquer].

What this family does not usually make explicit is *which* state a worker should return. The protocols above fix one schema per system. Our oracle instead has the root state the partial's shape and key space in the subtask itself, per question, and the merge is the arithmetic that shape implies.

## 4. Sequential harnesses: folding a memory over the input

The sequential family reads pieces in order and carries a running state. Chain of Agents chains worker agents, each reading one segment and passing a communication unit to the next, with a manager producing the final answer; it beat RAG, full-context, and multi-agent baselines by up to 10% on QA, summarization, and code completion [zhang2024coa]. RecurrentGPT simulated an LSTM with natural-language memories for arbitrarily long generation [zhou2023recurrentgpt]. MemGPT framed the same idea as an operating system paging content between the window and external storage [packer2023memgpt]. PRISM gives the running state a typed hierarchical schema, appends amendments instead of rewriting the memory so the KV cache stays valid, and reports 4x shorter contexts with up to 54% lower cost [jayalath2025prism]. COSMIR replaces free-form messages with a structured memory shared between a planner, iterating workers, and a manager, and beats Chain of Agents on HELMET QA [gupta2025cosmir].

This is also where training has advanced fastest. MemAgent trains a segment-by-segment memory agent with an overwrite strategy through multi-conversation RL (an extension of DAPO); trained on 32K inputs with an 8K window, it extrapolates to a 3.5M-token QA task with under 5% loss and exceeds 95% on RULER at 512K [yu2025memagent]. MEM1 learns a constant-size internal state for multi-turn tasks by RL [zhou2025mem1]. InfoMem replaces sparse answer rewards with an answer-conditioned information-gain reward on the memory itself [han2026infomem]. On the agent side, Context-Folding lets an agent branch into a sub-trajectory and fold it back into a summary, trained end-to-end with FoldGRPO, matching ReAct with a 10x smaller active context [sun2025contextfolding]; ACON optimizes compression guidelines from failure analysis [kang2025acon]; CompactionRL trains models to compact their own history mid-rollout [li2026compactionrl]; AdaCoM trains an external manager to edit a frozen agent's context [yi2026adacom].

Two observations from this family shape our design. First, the fold is order-preserving by construction, which makes it the right structure when the answer depends on order (variable tracking, running totals) and the wrong one when it does not, since it serializes work that could run in parallel and lets a long chain accumulate drift. Second, the trained sequential systems are trained on one aggregation structure and one task family, and MemAgent's headline result is length extrapolation within that family rather than transfer across families.

## 5. Navigational and agentic reading

A third family gives the model a map of the document and lets it decide where to look. MemWalker builds a tree of summaries and navigates it interactively per query [chen2023memwalker]. ReadAgent pages the document into episodes, compresses each to a gist, and looks up original pages when the gists are not enough, extending effective context 3.5 to 20x on QuALITY, NarrativeQA, and QMSum [lee2024readagent]. GraphReader structures the text as a graph and explores it with a 4K window, beating GPT-4-128K from 16K to 256K [li2024graphreader]. LongAgent has a leader direct member agents over chunks and adds an inter-member communication step to resolve conflicts caused by hallucination [zhao2024longagent]. These systems are strong on retrieval-style QA because the document has a small witness set; they are not designed for questions whose answer is a statistic over the whole input.

## 6. Recursive self-invocation

The closest family to our work lets the model call itself. Recursion of Thought trained a model to split a problem across multiple contexts using special tokens, solving arithmetic whose full solution ran to hundreds of thousands of tokens [lee2023rot]; Decomposed Prompting made the decomposition modular in prompting [khot2023decomposed]. Recursive Language Models (RLMs) revived the idea for long inputs: the prompt is stored as a variable in a Python REPL that the model can inspect, slice, and pass to recursive sub-calls of itself, so that inputs two orders of magnitude beyond the window can be processed [zhang2025rlm]. Against compaction, CodeAct with sub-calls, and Claude Code, RLMs reported median gains of 26%, 130%, and 13% across four benchmarks including OOLONG, and handled 6M to 11M tokens on BrowseComp-Plus. The paper also trains a small RLM: RLM-Qwen3-8B is Qwen3-8B fine-tuned by rejection-sampled SFT on about 1,000 filtered trajectories from Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B on LongBenchPro, 300 steps and 48 H100-hours, and improves 28.3% median over the base model on CodeQA, BrowseComp-Plus, OOLONG, and OOLONG-Pairs, approaching vanilla GPT-5 on three of them; training targets the root's REPL use and its decision of when to sub-call, not every recursion level.

Follow-ups arrived quickly. SRLM adds uncertainty-aware self-reflection (self-consistency, reasoning length, verbalized confidence) to the program search and gains up to 22% over RLM at equal time budget [alizadeh2026srlm]. λ-RLM replaces free-form recursive code with a typed functional runtime grounded in λ-calculus, winning 29 of 36 model-task comparisons with formal termination and cost guarantees [roy2026lambdarlm]. Recursive Agent Harnesses make the recursive unit a full agent with tools, spawning subagent harnesses in parallel from generated scripts, and lift OOLONG-synthetic from 71.75% to 81.36% with GPT-5 and to 89.77% with Claude Sonnet 4.5, without training [lumer2026rah]. Prime Intellect implemented RLM scaffolding in an RL environment framework and reported token-efficiency gains on DeepDive, math, OOLONG, and copying tasks, with training of recursion-native models as future work [primeintellect2026rlm]; alphaXiv then RL-trained a 4B model as a recursive language model with a single shared parent-child policy, where child rollouts inherit the parent's advantage normalized by the number of sub-calls, matching Claude Sonnet's rubric score on scientific evidence selection at 8x the speed [kim2026reinforcingrlm]. SearchSwarm trains delegation into the weights: a harness constrains a main agent to decompose and delegate to subagents within a bounded context, the harness-guided trajectories become SFT data, and the resulting 30B-A3B model leads its size class on BrowseComp [lan2026searchswarm]. Related agent-side work gives the model explicit awareness of its own context budget through typed addressable memory blocks [xu2026vista] or decentralizes coordination through a shared verified context and task queue [mao2026delm].

Practitioner reports point the same way with candid costs. Anthropic's research system uses an orchestrator with parallel subagents that each get their own context window, reports a 90.2% gain over a single agent on internal research evaluations, and notes that multi-agent runs use about 15x the tokens of a chat [anthropic2025multiagent]. Cognition argues the opposite for coding agents, that parallel subagents make conflicting implicit decisions and that shared full traces beat delegation [yan2025dontbuild]. Laban et al. quantify a related risk: across 52 professional domains, frontier models corrupt about 25% of a document's content by the end of a long editing workflow, with damage growing with file size and task count [laban2026delegate52].

## 7. The prompted-versus-trained axis, and what is trained

Sorting the harnesses above by what lives in the weights:

| trained component | works | supervision |
|---|---|---|
| nothing (prompted scaffold) | CoA, LLM×MapReduce, ToM, XpandA, COSMIR, MemWalker, ReadAgent, GraphReader, LongAgent, LC-Boost, PRISM, RLM (original), SRLM, λ-RLM, Recursive Agent Harnesses | none |
| task-specific recursive summarizer | Wu et al. 2021 | human comparisons, BC + reward model |
| recursion with special tokens (arithmetic) | Recursion of Thought | synthetic supervised traces |
| sequential memory update | MemAgent, MEM1, InfoMem | RL on final-answer or information-gain rewards |
| context compaction / folding in agents | Context-Folding, CompactionRL, AdaCoM, ACON (guidelines, no weights) | RL with process rewards; LLM-optimized prompts |
| root's REPL use and sub-call decisions | RLM-Qwen3-8B | SFT on trajectories distilled from a 480B model |
| decomposition and delegation | SearchSwarm | SFT on harness-guided trajectories |
| recursive parent/child policy | alphaXiv RL for RLMs | RL with shared policy, inherited advantage |

Three gaps stand out against this table.

- **No prior work trains the choice of aggregation structure.** The trained sequential systems are fold-only; the trained recursive systems inherit whatever structure the teacher trajectories used. Whether a question needs an order-preserving fold or admits a parallel tree-reduce is a property of the task, and none of the trained harnesses make the model state that property or choose accordingly.
- **Supervision is either expensive or noisy.** Human feedback (Wu et al.), distillation from a much larger model (RLM-Qwen3-8B, SearchSwarm), and RL with outcome rewards (MemAgent, InfoMem, FoldGRPO) each import either cost or the teacher's errors, and RL on aggregation tasks has known reward-sparsity problems that LongRLVR and InfoMem address with dense context rewards [chen2026longrlvr, han2026infomem]. Scripted oracles on synthetic tasks, as in Recursion of Thought and in our work, give exactly correct traces at zero labeling cost, at the price of having to design tasks whose structure transfers.
- **Evaluation is mostly in-family.** RLM-Qwen3-8B is trained on LongBenchPro and evaluated on OOLONG and BrowseComp-Plus, which is a real transfer, but the trajectories come from a teacher that already solved similar tasks. MemAgent's transfer is length extrapolation within QA. SearchSwarm trains and tests on deep research. Transfer of a trained decomposition policy to benchmark families it never saw during training, RULER's tracking and frequency tasks and OOLONG's aggregation tasks in our case, is not something the literature has measured.

## 8. Where our work sits

Our harness is the recursive self-invocation family without a code runtime: two tools, `read_chunk` and `spawn_subagent`, a per-agent budget fixed at 3,000 tokens regardless of document length, and the model itself as the entire policy. The root reads the question, states whether the result over a range depends on the order of the input, commits to either a recursive binary split with a stated partial-state contract or a left fold with a running accumulator, and writes the subtask each child receives. Internal nodes merge by the arithmetic the contract implies; leaves read text and compute.

Relative to the table above, the design choices that distinguish it are:

1. **Strategy as a stated task property.** The root's first decision is fold versus tree, made from the task's order dependence, with the reason written into the trace. Section 4's fold-only systems and Section 6's teacher-inherited structures do not make this choice explicit, and our own ablations across training runs (documented in `training_runs.md`) show the decision is sensitive to where in the root's turn it is committed.
2. **Scripted-oracle supervision on disjoint task families.** All traces come from oracles that execute the canonical decomposition on synthetic records, prose with planted structure, and real documents with mechanical questions; the evaluation families (RULER, OOLONG) are never trained on. This is the Recursion of Thought recipe scaled to a general harness, and the opposite of the distillation recipe used for RLM-Qwen3-8B and SearchSwarm.
3. **Constant per-agent context.** Depth scales with document length while each agent's context does not, so the model's short-context accuracy is the only accuracy that matters, which is the premise of Xu et al.'s analysis carried to its limit [xu2025divideconquer]. The same premise is why leaf density, not document length, is our binding constraint.
4. **Aggregation rather than retrieval as the target.** The retrieval-oriented families of Sections 2 and 5 are strong where a small witness set exists. Our held-out set weights OOLONG's counting, per-user, and temporal questions, which have none, alongside RULER's tracking and frequency tasks.

The closest published comparison points are RLM-Qwen3-8B (a small model taught a recursive harness by SFT, evaluated on OOLONG) and MemAgent (a small model taught a fold by RL, evaluated on RULER at long lengths). Neither trains the structure choice, and neither reports transfer to task families absent from training, which is the claim our evaluation is built to test.
